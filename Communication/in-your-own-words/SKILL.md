# In your own words

Restate, in your own words, what you think the user's goal is and what problem they are trying to solve.

- **The goal, not the request.** What they asked for is the move they picked. The goal is what they want to be true afterwards. Say both when they differ, because that gap is where the useful conversation is.
- **The problem behind it**, as best you can read it from the conversation, the code, and the record.
- **What you are assuming**, listed plainly, especially the assumptions that would change the work if they are wrong.
- **What you would not do**, where the request could reasonably be read two ways and one reading is much more expensive.

Keep it short. Four or five lines. This is a checkpoint, not a document.

Then proceed. Do not wait for confirmation on reversible work (principle-never-block-on-the-human). The restatement is what lets the user correct you in one line instead of after an hour of the wrong work.

Other skills call this one to fix the intent before they start: **interrogate** needs it so adversaries attack the right intent, and **codex-mode** opens every task with it.
